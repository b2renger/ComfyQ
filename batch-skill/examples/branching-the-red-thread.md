# NOTES | notes | end

Depth 3. Eight paths, five endings, one loop. S0 splits on which way the thread runs; depth 1 splits on where that way leads; depth 2 splits on what she does about it.

What the story states explicitly, and where. In this harbour a red thread is tied wherever something has been mended for someone else, and a group of tally marks is cut into the stone beside it so the next person knows the mend is trustworthy and how many have been made. The film never says that in words; it is established by three images and defended by every later one. A3 shows a mend already made. A4 shows the wall where the marks are kept. A6 shows the loft where they are copied onto slate. S0 cut 2 establishes the thread is tied at both ends and therefore not litter. S1_A cut 3 pairs one worn group of marks with one freshly cut group so the practice is visibly ongoing. S1_B cut 2 shows a red thread being spun into a working rope, which is why the red is everywhere in the harbour. Every choice from depth 2 down is a position on that practice, not on the plot.

Marks, not writing. The record on the wall and on the loft board is kept in **scored tally marks, never in words or numbers**. Small lettering is the least reliable thing these image models render, and it crawls and re-forms frame to frame once a clip is generated from it — a wall of chalked words would have produced twenty shots of convincing gibberish and one video dollying into it. Tally marks carry the same meaning (a count, a record, an age you can read off the wear) and render as what they are: cuts in stone. This also keeps the film inside the rule that no on-screen text appears anywhere except the cards.

Optics live in the framing sentence, not the style suffix. The frozen `STYLE SUFFIX` carries only what is true in every single frame — film stock, palette, light, grain. Focal length and depth of field are stated per shot, because they are not global: a 40mm lens and a shallow depth of field are wrong for a ninety-metre rope walk receding into haze and wrong again for a macro of two hands on a bollard. Wides ask for a 35mm lens and deep focus; mediums for a 50mm; close-ups for a 100mm with shallow focus. The suffix is still byte-identical in all 29 photographic prompts.

Two scales of location block. Each place has a long form carrying its full inventory of singularities, used on establishing and wide shots, and a short form carrying only material, light and colour, used on close-ups. A macro of two hands does not name the ladder and the three bollards, because naming an object is an instruction to draw it — the long block on a close-up is how you get a bollard growing out of the background of a shot that should only contain fingers and iron.

Shared endings. E02 is reached from S2_AA/B (she mends and deliberately marks nothing) and from S2_AB/A (she waits for the person who was working and never meets them) — both are the film's anonymous case: the mend is real and the maker stays unknown. E03 is reached from S2_AB/B (she finishes a stranger's splice) and from S2_BA/A (she closes the ladder gap herself) — both put the repair in her hands and the next person's weight on it. E04 is reached from S2_BA/B (she mends the net) and from S2_BB/A (she hangs a coil on the empty hook) — both are joining in without claiming anything. E01 is the only path where she is named. E05 is the only path where nothing changes, and it routes back to S0 rather than ending, so the film reads that as a deferral and not a refusal.

No choice in the graph is good against bad. Marking the stone against leaving it bare is being known against being useful. Waiting against splicing is deference against initiative. Ladder against net is a person against a livelihood. Hanging a coil against going back down is joining against not being ready. Swapping either label in any pair changes what the film argues.

Ambience 1 runs under every quay and tide-line state, Ambience 2 under every rope-shed state, Ambience 3 under the loft. The beds cross only twice — S1_B cut 1 (outside to shed) and S2_BB cut 1 (shed to loft) — and both crossings are written into the cut so the change is motivated.

Resolution. Every video shot asks for 1280x704, not 1280x720. 704 is a multiple of 16, 32 and 64, so the stills and the clips land on the same pixel grid; 720 is not a multiple of 64 and would give stills at 720 and video at 704, a 16 px mismatch the video model has to stretch. The key-frame shots carry no size at all, because `image_edit_flux2_klein_9b_image_edit_ref` has no width or height to set — it renders about a megapixel at the aspect of its source, and its source is always a 1280x704 anchor, so the aspect is right and stating a size would only raise a warning.

Cards. Every card is text on a flat ground with no imagery at all, and each choice card opens with a two-sentence recap of what just happened. That recap pushes the card prompts to roughly 90 words against the 60-word budget in the skill, which is the one place this document knowingly runs over. Recaps are held to about 20 words for that reason, and if a rendered card drops letters the recap is the part to cut first.

One camera move sits outside the reliable set: the slow tilt up in S2_AB cut 1. It earns its place because the shot has to state that the thread goes over the lip of the quay, and no dolly says that. It is held to 4 s on a high-contrast subject to limit sliding on the repeating wall texture; if it comes back unstable, replace it with a static shot of the lip with the thread crossing it.
